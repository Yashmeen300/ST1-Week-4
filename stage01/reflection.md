# SmartCare Stage 1 Reflection

Prior to utilising AI, I experimented with a simple SmartCare appointment prototype that kept track of appointment timings, practitioner names, and patient names. I then worked with an improved version that arranged appointment information using a list, dictionaries, and functions. This made it easier for me to see how a basic real-world system could be represented in Python.

I was able to detect constraints that were not immediately visible to me and see the function of various programme components with the use of AI. For instance, the programme verifies the patient's name but not the other details of the appointment. Conflicting appointments might be taken into consideration, according to AI.

Therefore, I discovered that AI recommendations shouldn't always turn into mandates. Before being approved, certain recommendations must be evaluated against the SmartCare standards since they could make assumptions about the client's preferences and wants.

I ran the programme and tested both typical and unusual inputs to confirm it. A typical appointment, appointments with the same practitioner and time, appointments with a blank patient name, and an appointment time of none were all tried. The remaining tasks included determining whether recommendations were suitable, testing the programme, comparing it to the clients wants and ensuring that I could explain the finished code on my own.
